The research behind teach-back

  • Create > consume

    Students learn more when they construct explanations than when they read or watch.

    ICAP framework — Chi & Wylie, 2014

  • 35%+

    Improved learning when students prepare to teach vs. standard study (g = 0.35, meta-analysis).

    Kobayashi, 2019

  • Higher recall

    Students expecting to teach remember and organise more.

    Nestojko et al., 2014

  • Video > text

    Students learn more creating audio-visual teaching materials than written ones.

    Ribosa & Duran, 2022 meta-analysis

No Learning-Pyramid or “90% retention” claims here — those aren’t evidence-based, and we’d rather you trust the numbers we do use.

Assessment in the age of AI

The oral exam, made scalable.

Universities are returning to oral examinations — not to catch cheating, but because polished written work no longer proves understanding, and AI detectors can't reliably tell the difference. When a student explains a topic, understanding is visible. A Fishbole teach-back brings that to every classroom: a 5-minute recorded explanation is an oral exam every student can sit — and every teacher has time to review.

More ways to show understanding

Not every student shows their best thinking in an essay. Teach-back gives students who struggle with writing — or who think out loud — a way to demonstrate real understanding through speaking, structure, and slides. Same rigour, different mode of expression.

What is a teach-back assignment?

A teach-back assignment asks students to research a topic and explain it clearly in their own words — in a short video presentation. Instead of submitting a written report, students demonstrate understanding by teaching the concept back to their teacher.

Frequently asked questions

Is it really capped at 5 minutes?

Yes — by design. The constraint forces clarity and keeps videos watchable.

Does Fishbole use AI?

No. No AI writing, no avatars, no generated scripts. Students present in their own voice. That's the point.

Is Fishbole a screen recorder?

No. Fishbole connects to Google Slides via the Slides API — slides stay live and editable even after sharing.

Can students submit privately?

Yes — submissions can be private to the teacher only.

What ages does this suit?

Upper primary through tertiary — the 5-minute format works at any level, with expectations adjusted by age.

How do we share to our LMS?

Link or embed — works alongside major LMS platforms.

Do we need to install anything?

No — it runs entirely in the browser.
